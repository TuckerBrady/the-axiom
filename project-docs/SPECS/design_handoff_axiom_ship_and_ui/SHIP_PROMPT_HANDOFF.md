# Ship design handoff — v3 (FINAL DIRECTION)

Direction locked after two rounds. **Grok's v2 interpretation is the base** —
its proportions, surface restraint and integrated form language won on feel,
which matters more than checkbox count. ChatGPT's v2 scored higher on the
literal checks but its ship reads as a bigger, busier vessel.

Two things carry over from ChatGPT's version: the **elongated belly pod** (not a
sphere) and the requirement that the **relay array read as precision work**.

Feed the reference images alongside this prompt if the tool allows it.

---

## The prompt

> Technical concept art of a spacecraft called **The Axiom** — a long-range
> military scout, **46 metres long**, hull registration **AX-07**. Show it
> **twice, one above the other, in flat side profile facing right**, labelled
> **BEFORE REPAIR** and **AFTER REPAIR**. Dark background, side elevation, no
> perspective drama. Cold, precise, technical-illustration feel.
>
> **Scale and proportion — this is a SMALL ship.** Compact and stubby, roughly
> **3.8 times longer than tall**. It is crewed by one person and one robot, and
> the proportions must say so — not a freighter, not a capital ship, not
> elongated. Short, dense, purposeful.
>
> **Construction — one integrated hull, not an assembly of bolted-on parts.**
> Flat faceted plating that reads as a single monolithic vessel. Equipment is
> **recessed into** the hull rather than perched on top of it. The hull is
> **deep at the stern and steps down and inward partway along its length** to a
> shallower forward section. The bow ends in a **blunt faceted wedge with a
> flat vertical nose face** — no point, no needle, no taper to a tip.
>
> **Restrained surface detail.** Clean, legible plating with large panel forms.
> This design must stay readable when reproduced small, so avoid dense
> greebling, heavy rust-brown weathering, and busy micro-detail. Worn and
> patched, but calm — not cluttered.
>
> **One oversized engine, not a pair.** A heavy rectangular drive block at the
> stern, bulkier and cruder than the hull, ending in a single squared thrust
> nozzle. No symmetrical nacelles, no wings, no fins.
>
> **The signal relay array — the most important detail.** A **low structure
> recessed into the spine**, set INTO the hull rather than mounted on top,
> sitting no higher than the bow. It is built from **fine precision-machined
> nested arcs and thin flat blade elements** — elegant, exact, clearly
> engineered to a higher standard than anything around it. **Not a mesh grille,
> not a wire screen, not a lattice tower, not a truss radio mast, not a
> perforated panel.** It must read as *finely machined*, unmistakably
> better-made than the crude hull it sits in. This contrast is the single most
> important thing in the image: it is the work of someone far more skilled than
> whoever maintained the rest of the ship.
>
> **An elongated rounded pod hangs from the belly** amidships — a long capsule
> form, not a sphere, protruding clearly below the hull line. A smaller rounded
> dome sits on the spine near the bow. These are the only two curved forms on
> the vessel; everything else is angular.
>
> **A low canopy of narrow windows** on the spine forward, just behind the nose.
>
> **The ship's history must be visible in its surface. On a quiet hull these
> should read clearly — do not let them dissolve into texture:**
> - A **rectangle of newer, mismatched grey paint** on the flank amidships,
>   **painted over the original hull registration** so `AX-07` is only
>   partially legible beneath it — ghosted, half-obscured, deliberately
>   suppressed. It must read as something covered up, not as a nameplate.
> - **Three clearly different welding styles at three separate seams**, well
>   spaced along the hull and unmistakably different from one another: one a
>   **crude hand-run zigzag**, one a **neat line of round rivets**, one a
>   **heavy stepped square-edged plate overlap**. Three different people
>   repaired this ship and none of them coordinated. All three must be
>   individually findable.
> - **An external copper-toned pipe** running along the forward hull, clamped on
>   by hand, conspicuously ignoring the hull's own panel lines.
> - **Two weapon hardpoints on the underside, plated over and cross-braced
>   shut** in copper-toned metal. Deliberately sealed, not battle-damaged.
> - **A plated battery bank** beneath the aft hull behind the belly pod — a
>   flat boxy module with external cable clamps.
>
> **BEFORE REPAIR:** dark, cold, entirely unlit. Every surface visible but
> nothing glowing. Grimy and long-neglected, worn by service and improvised
> repair — not wrecked, not burning, not covered in rust.
>
> **AFTER REPAIR — the difference must be dramatic.** The same ship, visibly
> cared for: grime reduced, plating cleaner, the ghosted registration slightly
> more legible. **Cool cyan light** in the relay array, the bridge canopy, the
> dorsal dome, the belly pod and the battery bank. **Hot amber glow** at the
> engine nozzle. **The two sealed weapon hardpoints stay completely dark and
> unlit in both images** — repaired, but never unlocked.
>
> **Palette:** near-black background. Cold steel blue-grey hull, dark slate
> fills, copper-orange only on the welds, the external pipe, the hardpoint
> plating and the cable clamps. Cyan and amber are the only light sources, and
> only in the AFTER image.
>
> **Deliberately asymmetric and unevenly plated** — the irregularity is the
> character — but keep the overall surface calm and readable.
>
> **Avoid:** elongated or capital-ship proportions, mesh grilles, wire screens,
> lattice towers, truss radio masts, sleek curves, chrome, gloss, twin engines,
> wings, fins, visible weapons, glass cockpit domes, lens flares, nebula
> backdrops, atmospheric haze, three-quarter hero angles, heavy rust
> weathering, dense greebling.

## What to judge on

Pass/fail first:

1. **Does it read as a small ship** — one pilot and a robot? Elongated is a
   restart.
2. **Is the relay array recessed into the spine and finely machined**, or is it
   a grille, screen or tower?
3. **Is the belly an elongated pod**, not a sphere?
4. **Is the registration painted over and half-illegible**, not lettered?
5. **Do the sealed hardpoints stay dark in the AFTER image?**

Then:

6. Is the array visibly finer work than the hull? Hardest thing to get, and the
   reason the structure exists.
7. Can you find all three weld styles separately?
8. Is the surface calm enough to survive being reproduced small?
9. Is the AFTER frame a transformation, or a color swap?

## Canon settled across rounds

- **Registration: AX-07.**
- **Length: 46 metres.**
- **Proportion: ~3.8:1.** Revised down from 4.6:1 — the longer ratio read as a
  freighter. Small-crew scale is part of the identity.
- **Relay array: recessed into the spine, precision-machined arcs.** Vertical
  towers rejected (20th-century naval). Mesh grilles rejected (reads as ordinary
  antenna hardware, loses the Maker's-hand contrast).
- **Belly: elongated pod.** A sphere reads as a pressure vessel; a pod reads as
  a compartment someone lives in.
- **Surface: restrained.** Practical constraint, not taste — the ship renders
  small in the hub on a phone, and dense detail turns to mud.

## Not settled — needs Tucker

ChatGPT's v2 sheet invented the tagline *"Built for distance. Kept alive by
people who shouldn't have to."* It is good, but it is copy, and copy needs
sign-off before it enters the game.
