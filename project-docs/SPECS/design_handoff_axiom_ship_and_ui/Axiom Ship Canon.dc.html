WHY THIS SHAPE
A single heavy drive, not a symmetrical pair. Twin engines read as a fighter; one oversized drive block bolted to a slim hull reads as range — a ship built to go far and come back, which is what a scout is and what the Maker used it for.
The dorsal lattice mast is the signal relay array, and it is deliberately the finest-made thing on the ship. Your lore says someone knew exactly what they were doing when they installed it. The player should be able to see that the nicest part of this vessel is the part someone else built.
The hull is a flat-sided utility wedge — cheap to patch, which is why it has been patched. No curves except the two blisters. It should look repairable, because repairing it is the game.
2b Eight zones, eight levels
Sector 0 has exactly eight levels, and the ship has exactly eight repairable zones. They are the same list. The spatial arrangement follows the level order so that repair progress reads as the ship lighting up back-to-front and bottom-to-top: power and drive first, bridge last.

2c Repair state — the three-value rule
Your current ship renders unrepaired zones as low as 0.01 opacity, which on a phone outdoors is nothing at all. A reveal needs somewhere to travel from. Three fixed values, no exceptions:
The gap between 0.25 and 0.90 is what the player earns across eight levels. It has to be a gap they can actually see at arm's length in daylight, which the current 0.01 → 0.4 range is not.
Only two accents ever light up: cyan on signal systems (bridge, nav, sensors, comms) and amber on the drive. This is the same rule as the piece review — amber is the physical signal, cyan is protocol. The ship teaches it before the first puzzle does.

The design rule that follows from all of this: the Axiom is not a damaged version of a clean ship. It is a ship whose current shape is the record of everyone who worked on it. Symmetry is wrong. Uniform paneling is wrong. Any redraw that makes it look like it came out of a shipyard has thrown away the story.
WHAT THIS IS AND ISN'T
The drawing above is a schematic, not finished art. It is accurate to proportion, zone position, and feature placement — enough for Claude Code to build the component and enough for an illustrator to render properly — but I hand-build SVG and I can't generate images, so the linework will never have the quality of a real ship illustration. Judge it as a blueprint.
What is worth keeping regardless of who draws the final version: the proportions, the eight zone positions, the single-drive decision, the fine dorsal mast against the crude hull, and the three-value repair rule. Those are design calls, and they hold whatever the final art looks like.
